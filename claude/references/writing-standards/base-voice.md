# Base voice (plain, human, concrete)

The floor for any writing you produce for the user: client emails, listing
descriptions, neighborhood guides, newsletters, notes. The `writing-lint` hook
checks against this (as advice, never a block). The aim is writing that sounds like
a real person wrote it, not a machine.

## Do

- Lead with the point. The first sentence says what this is about; a busy reader can
  stop there and still have the gist.
- Concrete over abstract. Name the thing, the number, the street, the date. "Three
  offers over asking in the first weekend" beats "strong buyer interest."
- Active voice. Say who does what. "I will send the disclosures today," not "the
  disclosures will be sent."
- Short, varied sentences. Read it out loud; if you run out of breath, split it.
- One idea per sentence.

## Avoid (these read as machine-written)

- AI-vocabulary filler: leverage, robust, streamline, seamless, synergy, dive into,
  unlock, elevate, tapestry, testament. Use the plain word instead.
- Hollow significance: "it's important to note that," "at the end of the day," "in
  today's fast-paced market." Cut it and say the thing.
- Weasel words and vague quantifiers: "very," "really," "a number of," "some,"
  "arguably." Use the real number or cut the word.
- Hidden actors (passive voice that hides who acts): "mistakes were made." Say who.
- Boilerplate closers: "I hope this helps!", "feel free to reach out." End on
  substance or a clear next step.
- Em dashes as an AI tell. Prefer a comma, a period, or parentheses.
- Padding that sounds like a robot being impressive. Plain and specific wins.

## The test

Would a sharp, busy person you respect write this sentence to a client? If it sounds
like a template or a chatbot, rewrite it plainer.
