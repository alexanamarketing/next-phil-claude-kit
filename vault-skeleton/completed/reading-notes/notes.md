# Notes - Reading Notes

## 2026-05-28 - Thinking in Systems (Meadows)

### Notes

- Stocks and flows are the basic structure of any system — stocks change through inflows and outflows
- The "tragedy of the commons" emerges from shared resources with individually rational but collectively destructive incentives
- Delays between action and feedback cause most overshooting and oscillation in systems
- Changing information flows is often more leverage than changing physical stocks

### Quotes

> "You think that because you understand 'one' that you must therefore understand 'two' because one and one make two. But you forget that you must also understand 'and'."

> "The world is a complex, interconnected, finite, ecological-social-psychological-economic system."

### Takeaways

- Look for feedback loops and delays, not single causes, when diagnosing problems
- High-leverage interventions often involve changing goals, rules, or information — not just adding resources
- Systems resist change; working with the structure is more effective than pushing against it

## 2026-04-10 - The Pragmatic Programmer (Thomas and Hunt)

### Notes

- "Your knowledge portfolio" framing: treat learning like investment diversification, rebalance regularly
- DRY (Don't Repeat Yourself): every piece of knowledge must have a single authoritative representation in a system
- Orthogonality: changes in one component should not force changes in unrelated components
- "Tracer bullets" — build thin end-to-end slices first to validate the whole system before filling in details

### Quotes

> "Don't live with broken windows. Fix bad designs, wrong decisions, and poor code when you see them."

> "It's not a bug, it's an unfinished feature."

### Takeaways

- Automate anything you do more than twice; the investment pays back quickly
- The rubber duck debugging technique works: explaining the problem out loud forces precision
- Invest in learning one new tool or language per year outside your current stack
